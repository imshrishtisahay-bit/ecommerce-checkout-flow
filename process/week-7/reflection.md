# Reflection

## What Surprised Me

I was surprised by how much planning is required beyond designing UI screens. I learned that usability testing, accessibility checks, spacing consistency, typography, A/B testing, and documenting prototype limitations are all essential parts of creating a user-centered checkout experience.

---

## What I Got Stuck On

I found Figma prototyping challenging, especially when handling multiple checkout scenarios such as payment failure, coupon validation, and shipping errors. I also struggled with Figma's limitations, including the lack of true keyboard navigation, real input interactions, and dynamic branching within a single prototype.

---

## If I Were to Redo It

I would build the design system first by defining typography, spacing, and reusable components before designing the screens. I would also plan all user flows and edge cases in advance, making the prototype more organized and consistent. Additionally, I would explore converting the Figma prototype into HTML/CSS to implement interactions that are not possible in Figma alone.
